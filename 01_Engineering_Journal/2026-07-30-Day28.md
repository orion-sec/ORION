# ORION Engineering Journal

## Day 28 – Introducing the Evidence Reasoning Layer

### Objective

The objective for today was to extend ORION's investigation architecture beyond evidence collection by introducing a dedicated Evidence Reasoning Layer.

Until now, ORION could collect structured evidence from threat intelligence providers. Today's work introduced the ability for ORION to independently interpret that evidence and generate its own findings.

This separates observed facts from ORION's own reasoning.

---

## Work Completed

### 1. Introduced the Finding Model

Created a standardized Finding object.

Unlike Evidence objects, which represent observed facts collected from external data sources, Finding objects represent ORION's interpretation of those facts.

Example:

{
    "category": "Infrastructure",
    "finding": "The investigated IP appears to originate from hosted or data-centre infrastructure."
}

This establishes a clear distinction between evidence and reasoning.

---

### 2. Created the Evidence Reasoning Module

Created:

11_Modules/evidence_reasoning.py

The module currently provides two standardized functions:

- create_finding()
- reason_over_evidence()

This creates a dedicated architectural layer responsible solely for reasoning over structured evidence.

---

### 3. Integrated the Reasoning Layer

Updated threat_engine.py to import:

from evidence_reasoning import reason_over_evidence

The Threat Correlation process now performs the following sequence:

Threat Results

↓

Evidence Collection

↓

Evidence Reasoning

↓

Threat Correlation

↓

Operational Decision

Although the reasoning layer currently contains a single rule, the architecture now supports future reasoning expansion without affecting downstream modules.

---

### 4. Implemented ORION's First Reasoning Rule

Added ORION's first reasoning capability.

When Infrastructure evidence is observed, ORION now produces an Infrastructure finding describing the significance of that evidence.

This represents the first time ORION generated knowledge that was not directly supplied by an external threat-intelligence provider.

---

### 5. Validation

Executed the complete investigation pipeline.

Validation confirmed:

- 12 pipeline stages executed successfully
- Zero failures
- Evidence objects remained unchanged
- Findings were successfully generated
- Threat Correlation now returns both evidence and findings
- No downstream modules required modification

---

## Lessons Learned

Evidence and reasoning are separate responsibilities.

Evidence answers:

"What facts do we know?"

Reasoning answers:

"What do those facts mean?"

Separating these responsibilities improves maintainability, simplifies future reasoning expansion, and creates a cleaner architecture for AI-assisted investigations.

---

## End of Day Reflection

Today's milestone represents another significant architectural improvement for ORION.

The platform no longer only reports information gathered from external providers.

ORION now produces its own structured findings based on collected evidence.

This marks the beginning of ORION's reasoning capability and establishes a scalable foundation for future threat interpretation.
