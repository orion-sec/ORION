# ORION Engineering Journal

## Day 33 – Decision Engine v1.0 Completed

Date:
04 August 2026

---

# Objective

Today's objective was to transform ORION from a cognitive reasoning pipeline into a complete investigation decision platform capable of producing deterministic investigation outcomes and analyst-ready reporting.

Unlike previous days, the focus shifted from building individual data models to implementing the complete reasoning workflow used by experienced SOC analysts and Incident Responders.

---

# Work Completed

## 1. Explainable Confidence Engine

Implemented a weighted confidence calculation engine capable of combining multiple investigation signals into a single explainable confidence score.

Signals currently supported include:

- Evidence Strength
- Identity Risk
- Threat Intelligence Risk
- Hypothesis Support
- Business Context Risk
- Historical Behaviour Risk
- Detection Quality

Each signal contributes to the final confidence score using configurable weighting rather than fixed thresholds.

This gives ORION transparent decision-making instead of simple confidence copying.

---

## 3. Decision Engine Refactoring

Refactored the complete Decision Engine.

Introduced the reusable helper:

build_decision()

This centralized all investigation outcome creation into a single implementation.

Benefits:

- Eliminated duplicated code
- Standardized investigation outputs
- Simplified future maintenance
- Reduced implementation complexity

The only remaining direct create_outcome() call exists for scenarios where no hypotheses are available.

# Lessons Learned

Separating reasoning from execution significantly improves architecture.

The platform now understands:

What happened

Why it happened

Why the decision was reached

What actions should be taken

Actual execution of containment actions will be implemented later through Microsoft Graph, Defender XDR, Sentinel, CrowdStrike and other APIs.
